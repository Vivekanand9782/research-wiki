## Title & Metadata
- **Full Paper Title**: Identification of loci for pre-harvest sprouting resistance in the highly dormant spring wheat RL4137
- **Authors**: M. M. Uzzal A. Liton, Curt A. McCartney, Colin W. Hiebert, Santosh Kumar, Mark C. Jordan, Belay T. Ayele
- **Year**: 2020
- **Journal**: Theoretical and Applied Genetics
- **DOI**: 10.1007/s00122-020-03685-y

## Abstract Summary
This study investigated [[pre-harvest sprouting]] (PHS) resistance in a doubled haploid (DH) population of 330 lines derived from a cross between the PHS-susceptible cultivar 'Roblin' and the highly dormant, PHS-resistant breeding line [[RL4137]]. Phenotyping was conducted using the [[speed of germination index]] (SGI) across five environments, and genotyping was performed with the wheat [[90K Infinium iSelect Custom Wheat Beadchip]]. Five quantitative trait loci (QTLs) were detected on linkage groups 1D, 4A.2, 6B.1, 6D, and 7A. The major QTL [[QPhs.umb-4A]] was highly consistent, explaining 40–50% of phenotypic variation, with the resistant allele contributed by [[RL4137]]. A novel minor QTL, [[QPhs.umb-1D]], was identified with the resistant allele contributed by 'Roblin'. Pyramiding these QTLs incrementally increased [[seed dormancy]], demonstrating that combining alleles from both parents significantly enhances PHS resistance in spring wheat.

## Introduction & Background
Hexaploid wheat ([[Triticum aestivum]] L.) is a critical global staple crop, but its productivity is significantly limited by [[pre-harvest sprouting]] (PHS). PHS refers to the germination of grains on the spike before harvest under wet and humid conditions, causing severe losses in grain yield and quality. Domestication selected against strong [[seed dormancy]] to ensure rapid, uniform germination, leaving modern cultivars highly susceptible to PHS. Consequently, breeding programs aim to introduce intermediate seed dormancy.

PHS is a complex, quantitatively inherited trait regulated by genetic and environmental factors. Previous studies identified QTLs across all 21 wheat chromosomes, with major loci characterized on group 3 chromosomes and chromosome 4AL, including genes like [[TaPHS1]], [[TaMKK3-A]], [[TaVP1]], and [[TaMyb10]]. Physical spike characteristics can also affect water penetration, making threshed-seed assays like the [[speed of germination index]] (SGI) essential for isolating physiological seed dormancy.

The spring wheat breeding line [[RL4137]] is a widely used source of PHS resistance, particularly in Canadian breeding programs. This study aims to determine the genetic basis of seed dormancy in [[RL4137]] using a large doubled haploid (DH) population crossed with the susceptible cultivar 'Roblin' to identify stable QTLs and tightly linked markers for marker-assisted selection.

## Key Concepts & Theory
- **[[Pre-harvest sprouting]] (PHS)**: The germination of wheat grains on the spike prior to harvest, triggered by wet and humid conditions, which degrades grain quality.
- **[[Seed dormancy]]**: An internal block that prevents intact, viable seeds from germinating under otherwise favorable environmental conditions.
- **[[Speed of germination index]] (SGI)**: A precise phenotyping metric calculated from daily germination counts over 21 days, isolating physiological dormancy from physical spike barriers.
- **[[Quantitative trait loci]] (QTL)**: Genomic regions associated with a quantitative trait, identified via statistical association with molecular markers.
- **[[Pyramiding]]**: The breeding strategy of combining multiple resistance alleles or QTLs into a single genotype to achieve cumulative phenotypic effects.
- **[[Transgressive segregation]]**: The appearance of individuals in a segregating population that exhibit phenotypes extreme or superior to either parent.

## Methods & Experimental Design
- **Population Development**: A mapping population of 330 doubled haploid (DH) lines was generated from a cross between 'Roblin' and [[RL4137]] using the maize pollination method.
- **Field and Greenhouse Trials**: The population was grown in five environments over 3 years: a greenhouse in 2016 (GH16), and field sites in Winnipeg (WPG17), Brandon (BRD17), and Morden (MOR17, MOR18). Field trials used alpha lattice designs with 3 replications; greenhouse trials used a randomized complete block design with 3 replications. Seeds were harvested at harvest maturity.
- **Phenotyping**: Physiologically mature threshed seeds were subjected to a 21-day germination test in Petri plates under darkness at room temperature. Daily germination counts were used to calculate the [[speed of germination index]] (SGI).
- **Genotyping**: DNA from 316 DH lines was genotyped using the [[90K Infinium iSelect Custom Wheat Beadchip]]. After filtering monomorphic and low-quality SNPs, 8,751 polymorphic SNPs were used for linkage mapping.
- **Linkage Mapping**: Linkage bins were determined using [[QTL IciMapping V4.1]]. Map construction was performed with [[MapDisto v1.7.7]] using a minimum LOD of 4.0 and maximum recombination fraction of 0.25, resulting in a framework map of 1,248 markers across 37 linkage groups.
- **Statistical and QTL Analysis**: Analysis of variance (ANOVA) and broad-sense heritability ($H^2$) were calculated using [[META-R]]. QTL mapping was performed with [[QTL IciMapping V4.1]] using Interval Mapping (IM) and Inclusive Composite Interval Mapping (ICIM). Significance thresholds were determined via 10,000 permutations (LOD thresholds: 3.13 for IM, 3.14 for ICIM).

## Key Results & Data
- **Phenotypic Variation**: SGI values varied across environments, with the highest population mean in WPG17 (14.44) and the lowest in MOR18 (12.68). 'Roblin' consistently showed high SGI (16.95–19.93), while [[RL4137]] SGI ranged from 1.90 (GH16) to 6.86 (BRD17). Broad-sense heritability across all five environments was high ($H^2 = 0.91$). SGI frequency distributions were skewed toward susceptibility, showing bimodal tendencies in some environments (e.g., WPG17).
- **Environmental Correlation**: Positive and highly significant correlations ($P < 0.001$) were observed among all environments, ranging from $r = 0.62$ (GH16 vs. WPG17) to $r = 0.74$ (GH16 vs. BRD17). ANOVA showed significant effects for genotype, environment, and genotype-by-environment interactions ($P < 0.001$).
- **Linkage Map**: The final map spanned 2388.6 cM. The B genome had the highest marker density (4,720 SNPs), followed by the A genome (2,855 SNPs) and the D genome (1,145 SNPs).
- **QTL Detection**:
  - [[QPhs.umb-4A]] (Linkage group 4A.2, ~55 cM): Detected in all environments. It explained 40–50% (up to 51.84% in pooled analysis) of phenotypic variation, with LOD scores up to 65.45. The resistance allele was contributed by [[RL4137]].
  - [[QPhs.umb-1D]] (Linkage group 1D, 142.5 cM): A novel, stable minor QTL detected in three environments and pooled data, explaining 1.99–2.33% of phenotypic variation. The resistance allele was contributed by 'Roblin'.
  - [[QPhs.umb-6B]] (Linkage group 6B.1, ~115–117 cM): Detected in four environments, explaining 3.09–4.33% of variation. The resistance allele was contributed by [[RL4137]].
  - [[QPhs.umb-6D]] (Linkage group 6D, ~1.6–3.7 cM): Stable minor QTL detected in pooled data and three environments, explaining 1.62–2.45% of variation. The resistance allele was contributed by [[RL4137]].
  - [[QPhs.umb-7A]] (Linkage group 7A, 73.4 cM): Detected only in MOR17, explaining 3.97–5.69% of variation.
- **QTL Pyramiding**: Combining the four stable QTLs incrementally increased dormancy. The major QTL [[QPhs.umb-4A]] alone reduced SGI by 32% (pooled data). Combining all four QTLs (genotypic class R1R2R3R4) reduced SGI by up to 53% compared to the fully susceptible class (S1S2S3S4).

## Mechanistic Insights
- The major QTL [[QPhs.umb-4A]] is located on chromosome arm 4AL (linkage group 4A.2) and is flanked by markers [[BS00068243_51]] and [[BS00037019_51]]. BLAST analysis of these flanking markers against the Chinese Spring RefSeq v1.0 genome placed them near 603.067 Mbp.
- This genomic region corresponds closely to the physical positions of two previously characterized seed dormancy genes: [[TaMKK3-A]] (located at 609.074 Mbp) and [[TaPM19]] (located at 604.056 Mbp). This suggests that [[QPhs.umb-4A]] is likely controlled by the same underlying pre-harvest sprouting regulator gene(s).
- The novel QTL [[QPhs.umb-1D]] is flanked by markers [[BS00022188_51]] and [[Kukri_c10485_1346]], which physically map to 462.203 Mbp and 475.724 Mbp on chromosome 1D, respectively.
- Mechanistically, [[QPhs.umb-4A]] acts as a primary genetic switch required for strong dormancy in this population. The minor QTLs [[QPhs.umb-1D]], [[QPhs.umb-6B]], and [[QPhs.umb-6D]] have minimal individual phenotypic effects in the absence of the resistant [[RL4137]] allele at [[QPhs.umb-4A]]. Instead, they function as genetic modifiers that fine-tune the level of seed dormancy, incrementally reducing the speed of germination when pyramided together with [[QPhs.umb-4A]].
- The contribution of the resistant allele for [[QPhs.umb-1D]] by the susceptible parent 'Roblin' indicates complementary gene action, where both parents contribute positive alleles to maximize pre-harvest sprouting resistance.

## Conclusions & Implications
This study successfully mapped the genetic architecture of [[pre-harvest sprouting]] (PHS) resistance in the highly dormant spring wheat line [[RL4137]]. The identification of five QTLs, particularly the major locus [[QPhs.umb-4A]] and the novel locus [[QPhs.umb-1D]], provides critical targets for wheat breeding. The major QTL [[QPhs.umb-4A]] is essential for establishing strong dormancy, while the minor QTLs on 1D, 6B, and 6D act additively to fine-tune the trait.

The discovery that the PHS-susceptible cultivar 'Roblin' contributes the resistance allele at [[QPhs.umb-1D]] highlights the value of utilizing complementary parental alleles. Pyramiding all four stable QTLs (on 1D, 4A, 6B, and 6D) can reduce the speed of germination by up to 53%, offering a highly effective strategy for breeders to systematically improve PHS resistance in spring wheat cultivars. Tightly linked SNP markers identified in this study can be directly integrated into marker-assisted selection programs to facilitate the development of PHS-resistant wheat varieties.

## Limitations & Caveats
- The phenotypic evaluation of PHS resistance was based solely on the [[speed of germination index]] (SGI) of threshed seeds. While this method precisely isolates physiological [[seed dormancy]], it does not account for non-dormancy physical barriers such as spike morphology, spike erectness, or glume tenacity, which also influence PHS in field conditions.
- The minor QTLs [[QPhs.umb-6B]] and [[QPhs.umb-6D]] were flanked by different SNP markers across different environments, which may complicate marker-assisted selection for these specific loci.
- The novel QTL [[QPhs.umb-1D]] and the minor QTLs explained relatively small percentages of phenotypic variation (1.62% to 4.33%), meaning their individual effects are minor and highly dependent on the presence of the major [[QPhs.umb-4A]] allele.
- The genetic map had very low marker coverage on chromosome 4D (only 8 SNPs mapped across two linkage groups), potentially limiting the ability to detect QTLs on this chromosome.

## Future Directions
- Fine-mapping and functional characterization of the novel QTL [[QPhs.umb-1D]] to identify the underlying causal gene(s) regulating [[seed dormancy]] on chromosome 1D.
- Validation of the identified QTLs and their linked SNP markers in diverse genetic backgrounds and commercial spring wheat breeding programs to confirm their stability and breeding utility.
- Investigation of the potential interaction between physiological dormancy QTLs (such as [[QPhs.umb-4A]]) and physical spike morphological traits to optimize overall field-level PHS resistance.
- Comparative genomic analysis to determine if [[QPhs.umb-4A]] is indeed driven by [[TaMKK3-A]], [[TaPM19]], or other novel candidate genes in the [[RL4137]] genetic background.
